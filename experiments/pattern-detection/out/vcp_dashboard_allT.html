Before you believe any of this

The strategy underperforms simply buying and holding the same universe (22.9% CAGR, but at a 46.7% drawdown) and its case is risk-adjusted, not absolute.
  • Four years carry everything. 2017, 2020, 2021 and 2023 produce essentially the whole return; the other seven are mildly negative, including the two most recent. A 19.8% CAGR is the average of a very lumpy series, not an income.
  • Selective, so a lot of the book is idle. The CAGR is computed on the full ₹10L including cash that was never deployed, which is honest but understates what the invested money did. The deployed-capital return is much higher — and so is the return of simply holding an index fund with the rest.
  • One trade in four wins. Profit factor holds up only because the average winner is several times the average loser. Taking the signals selectively is how you end up with the losers and none of the winners.
  • Survivorship bias. The universe is today's Nifty 500 members held back to 2016, which flatters both the strategy and the benchmark.
  • In-sample. Detector thresholds came from a synthetic benchmark, but the entry policy and the 50 EMA exit were both chosen by looking at this data.
  • A bull market. A slow trailing stop flatters itself in one, and 2016–2026 on the NSE was one.
  • No liquidity or impact model. Fills assume the close, and gapped stops assume the open.
  • No regime filter and no ranking of same-day candidates — ranking was tested and made no reliable difference.
