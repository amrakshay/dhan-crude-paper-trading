1. WHAT THE SAMPLE CONTAINED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A backtest with no disaster in it is undersampled, not safe. Before any
performance figure, this is how many chances it had to go wrong.

  sessions                        2,642
  single-day falls over 3%        20
  single-day falls over 5%        7
  single-day rises over 3%        18
  worst single session            -12.98%  (2020-03-23)
  worst 20-session stretch        -37.01%  (to 2020-03-23)

  7 sessions fell more than 5%: 7 independent chances for the
  strategy to meet the event that decides it, out of 2,642.
  Any conclusion about the tail rests on 7 observations.

5. RUIN RISK -- what size does the OBSERVED tail support?
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Resampled in whole months so that clustered losses stay clustered.
The question is not 'what is the expected return' but 'at what size
does a plausible sequence of these trades end the book'.

  return on MARGIN   : mean    -6.94%   worst   -666.88%   p5  -236.86%
  return on MAX LOSS : mean    -2.13%   worst   -100.52%   p5   -69.58%

  THE TWO BASES DISAGREE, AND THAT IS THE POINT. Sizing by MARGIN is
  what a broker's screen invites, and it is incoherent here: margin runs
  at a median of ~26% of the structure's own maximum loss, so the worst
  trade lost -667% of what it was blocking. You cannot lose
  that and stay solvent at any meaningful size. Measured against MAX
  LOSS the worst trade is -101%, bounded by construction -- and
  max loss IS the amount at risk for a defined-risk structure.
  Size on that, never on margin.

  'ruin' is ending below 20% of the starting book over five years.
  Read the last two columns, not the first.

  The MAX LOSS table is the meaningful one, and it says what the
  headline says: on this sample the strategy has a NEGATIVE expectancy
  after costs, so every size loses money and larger sizes lose it
  faster. Position sizing cannot rescue a negative edge; it only
  decides how long the book takes to get there.

  CAVEAT, and it is the important one: this resamples a tail observed
  over 2,642 sessions containing 7 days worse than -5%. The true tail is
  almost certainly worse than the one that happened to fall inside this
  window, so every ruin probability here is a LOWER BOUND.
