======================================================================================
WHY DOES A TRADE WITH A -2% STOP LOSE 40%?
======================================================================================

1. HOW LONG DID THE BIG LOSSES TAKE?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   DTE  trades  lost >10%   in ONE session  mean stop slip     worst
     7    1438        146       100 ( 68%)          -9.79%    -86.4%
    14    1190         66        41 ( 62%)          -6.79%    -32.1%
    30     913         40        20 ( 50%)          -5.61%    -30.6%
    45     658         12         8 ( 67%)          -5.07%    -73.0%

  Half to two-thirds of them happen in a SINGLE trading session, and the
  stop's slippage halves as the entry moves further from expiry. That
  gradient is gamma, and it points at the cause.

2. THE CAUSE -- the loss zone is narrower than a day's move
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
   DTE  short strike  long strike  loss zone  zone / 1-day sigma
     7        -2.22%       -4.31%      2.09%               2.38x
    14        -3.07%       -5.96%      2.89%               3.29x
    30        -4.37%       -8.23%      3.86%               4.39x
    45        -5.58%      -10.52%      4.95%               5.62x

  NIFTY's median daily sigma over this sample is 0.88% -- NSE's own
  published figure, the same one the margin model uses.

  At 7 DTE the entire distance from full profit to MAXIMUM loss is about
  2.4 standard deviations. The sample contains 20 sessions that fell
  more than 3% and 7 that fell more than 5%. On any of those the spread
  crosses the whole zone between one close and the next.

  A -2% stop sits INSIDE that zone, near the top. There is almost no
  room between the level at which the stop triggers and the level at
  which the position is already at its maximum loss.

3. THE WORST TRADE, TRACED END TO END
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  entered 2026-01-16, expiry 2026-01-20, forward 25,717
  SHORT 25450 PE  /  LONG 25200 PE   (gap 250 points = 0.97% of the index)
  credit Rs 1,060   maximum loss Rs 15,190

    2026-01-19  NIFTY  25,585.50   mark       -270  (  -1.8% of max loss)
    2026-01-20  NIFTY  25,232.50   mark    -13,078  ( -86.1% of max loss)

  settlement 25,232.50: short 25450 finished 217.50 in the money, long 25200 finished 0.00 in the money
  net Rs -13,127 = -86.4% of maximum loss

  THE STOP NEVER HAD A CHANCE TO FIRE. At the previous close the
  position was 1.8% underwater -- comfortably inside the -2% stop --
  because the index was still 135 points ABOVE the short strike with
  a day to run. The next observation was settlement.

  NIFTY fell 1.38% that session. The loss zone was 0.97% wide. The
  day's move was 1.4x the entire distance from fine to ruined.

4. AND NEAR EXPIRY THE MARK UNDERSTATES THE OUTCOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  The mark-to-market a stop watches is an EXPECTATION over what could
  still happen. Settlement is one draw from it. With a day to run and
  the index just above the short strike, the expectation is mildly
  negative -- the position might still expire worthless -- while the
  realisation is either fine or nearly the whole loss.

  So a stop placed on the mark is watching the wrong variable at exactly
  the moment it matters most. This is not fixable by moving the stop:
  a tighter one fires more often on noise, and a looser one gives up
  more before it fires. It is fixable by not being there -- which is
  what 'exit at 10 DTE' does, and why that was the best management rule
  in SWEEP.md section 5.
