  Two trades out of sixty. In several regimes they are the difference
  between a profitable strategy and an unprofitable one, and in none of
  them does the win rate move enough to notice. THAT is the shape of
  this strategy family, and it is why ruin risk rather than expectancy
  is the decision variable.

  a crash drives at least one trade to its maximum loss: yes  (3.3% of trades)
     note it is only ~2 trades in 60. A single crash in a five-year
     sample barely moves any AGGREGATE statistic, which is exactly
     why aggregates are the wrong way to judge this strategy.
  a volatility expansion reduces the mean:              yes
     the seller keeps quoting a stale implied volatility into a
     regime that has already repriced -- the realistic failure.
  fat tails produce a worse single trade:               yes
  a strong gap-heavy trend reduces the mean:            yes
     direction, not volatility, is what breaches a condor over and
     over -- and each new position is sold into the same trend.

  The 'faults' column counts sessions where a LIVE position could not be
  marked because a leg was outside the synthetic strike grid. On real NSE
  data it is structurally zero -- strikes are never delisted. Any non-zero
  here is a limit of the synthetic harness, not of the engine, and it is
  printed rather than hidden because it truncates the very tail the
  crash regime exists to produce.

  Read the win-rate column and then ignore it. It barely moves between
  a regime that pays and one that does not, which is the whole reason
  a high win rate is never evidence for this family of strategies.

  LEVEL 3: PASS
