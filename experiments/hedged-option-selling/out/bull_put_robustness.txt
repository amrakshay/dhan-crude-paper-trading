====================================================================================
IS THE BULL PUT SPREAD REAL, OR A CELL PICKED OUT OF A GRID?
====================================================================================
It is the only defined-risk structure in the sweep that made money.
That is exactly what a selection artefact looks like, so it gets tested
harder than the rest.

'R/risk /yr' is the annualised return per rupee-year of MAXIMUM LOSS --
the capital actually at risk. The comparator is the risk-free rate,
5.64% a year over this sample.

WHY IT MIGHT BE REAL, stated before the numbers so it is a prediction
rather than a rationalisation: on an equity index the volatility smile
is skewed, so the put sold sits on a steeper part of it than the put
bought. Measured on this data (NIFTY 2025-08-01, 27 DTE) the 16-delta
put carried 12.8% implied volatility against the 16-delta call's 10.6%.
A put spread is therefore short the richer option and long the cheaper
one in volatility terms, and a call spread is the reverse. If that is
the mechanism, the BEAR CALL spread should be badly negative -- and it
is, at -102% a year on capital at risk.

  BOTH HALVES ARE POSITIVE (21.7% and 29.6% a year).
  That is what a structural premium should look like, and it is
  the single strongest thing in favour of this result. It was NOT
  true before the expiry-selection fix: the first half then read
  -5.3%, because sessions were silently skipped whenever the
  nearest-to-target expiry was an illiquid weekly. See
  EXPIRY_MIX.md -- the objection was an artefact of the bug.

  Note separately that removing the worst three trades moves the total
  substantially -- a handful of observations still carry the result,
  whichever way the halves fall.

  A 20% chance that the true expectation is negative is not
  significance. It is a signal worth naming and not a result worth
  trading, and no amount of further parameter search will fix that
  -- searching harder on the same 2,643 sessions only makes the
  selection problem worse.

====================================================================================
VERDICT
====================================================================================
The put-side bias is REAL and theory-consistent: the same mechanism that
makes the bull put spread the best structure makes the bear call spread
the worst, and the sign of that difference was predicted before it was
measured. It survives both halves of the sample, the removal of 2020,
and eight times the assumed slippage.

  BUT THE CURRENT REGIME IS NEGATIVE. Since SEBI's index-derivatives
  framework took effect on 2024-11-20 the same rules return
  -29.1% a year against +43.3% before it -- on only 48
  trades, which is far too few to conclude from, but it is the
  period that describes trading this today. Larger lots, one weekly
  expiry per exchange, +2% expiry-day ELM and an STT rate that rose
  again in April 2026 all land in that window. This is now the
  strongest single objection, and it replaced an earlier one (the
  half-split) that turned out to be a bug artefact.

The MAGNITUDE is weaker evidence than the direction:

  * 2 of 31 variants are at or below zero (    DTE 7, after SEBI 2024-11-20);
  * 20% of bootstrap resamples lose money, and the 5th
    percentile is Rs -112,795;
  * the result still rests on a small number of trades -- removing the
    worst three moves it materially;
  * and the whole thing is one market, one underlying, 2,643 sessions
    containing seven days worse than -5%.

What this supports is 'index put skew is worth something after costs on
this sample', not 'here is a strategy'. Establishing the magnitude needs
data this sample does not contain -- more sessions, not more searching.
